# The Beautiful Tragedy of Fate

This [1983](https://www.history.com/a-year-in-history/1983)  Fantasy '[Krull](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0085811/)' Still Breaks My Heart.
![[articles/pop-culture/movies/krull-1983-1792043849190.webp|700x391]]

When I revisit the 1983 sci-fi fantasy epic [Krull](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0085811/), I don’t just see a nostalgic relic of 80s practical effects, laser swords, and giant spiders. I see a haunting, deeply melancholic meditation on mortality. Beneath its swashbuckling surface lies a profound emotional core, anchored by one of the most tragic and beautiful characters in cinematic history: [[rell-the-cyclops-from-movie-krull|Rell The Cyclops]]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ndpV6qfQJVw)

### The Quest for Love in a Dying World

For those who haven't seen it, the plot follows Prince Colwyn ([Ken Marshall](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0551362/)), whose kingdom is invaded by an alien entity known as the Beast. The Beast kidnaps Colwyn’s bride, Princess Lyssa ([Lysette Anthony](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000762/)), because a prophecy states that their union will produce a son capable of defeating him. Colwyn embarks on a desperate, seemingly impossible quest to find the mystical Glaive and storm the Black Fortress before the Beast can consume his bride.

Along the way, he gathers a ragtag band of misfits, including the bumbling magician Ergo ([David Battley](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0061035/)), the wise but doomed old guide Ynyr ([Freddie Jones](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0429054/)), and a group of outlaws that includes a young [Liam Neeson](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000553/) and the late, great [Robbie Coltrane](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0183393/).

But it is the inclusion of Rell ([Bernard Bresslaw](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0105348/)) that elevates the film from a simple adventure to a poignant philosophical tragedy.

### The Curse of Knowing

Rell belongs to an ancient race of [cyclopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclopes) who, centuries ago, made a fateful bargain with the Beast. They traded one of their eyes for the gift of foresight. But the Beast, in his cruelty, twisted the gift. The [cyclopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclopes) can only see one future event: the exact moment of their own death.

This lore is what makes Rell such a devastatingly beautiful character. It creates a heartbreaking duality in his existence.

On one hand, the [cyclopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclopes) are the bravest warriors in the realm. When they go into battle, if their inner vision does not show them dying in that specific fight, they fight with absolute, fearless abandon. They know they are immortal in that moment.

On the other hand, they are the saddest creatures alive. They carry the heavy, inescapable weight of their expiration date. They cannot change it, and trying to avoid it only brings them greater suffering. Because of this, when their time approaches, they do not rage against the dying of the light. They quietly accept it, often withdrawing into deep solitude to face the end alone, sparing their loved ones the agony of watching them fade.

### A Stoic Embrace of the End

What moves me to tears every time I watch [Krull](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0085811/) is Rell’s quiet dignity. In a world where humans are terrified of death, clinging to life and bargaining for just one more day, Rell represents a stoic grace. He doesn't panic. He doesn't weep. He simply waits for his moment, and when it arrives, he meets it with open arms.

This culminates in the film’s breathtaking climax. As the heroes try to breach the impenetrable Black Fortress, the massive doors begin to crush them. Rell realizes his foreseen moment has arrived. Without a word of complaint, he steps into the crushing gears, using his massive body to hold the doors open just long enough for Colwyn and the others to slip inside.

He dies alone, in the dark, fulfilling a prophecy he has seen since childhood. But in his sacrifice, he gives his friends the one thing he could never have: a future.

### Why It Matters Today

In our modern world, we suffer from what I call the "infinite time bias." We act as if our days are endless, delaying our dreams and taking our loved ones for granted. We hide from the reality of our own ticking clocks.

Rell the Cyclops forces us to confront the truth. He reminds us that our time is strictly limited, and that the awareness of our death shouldn't paralyze us with fear—it should ignite our courage. If we truly understood the fragility of our time, we would love more fiercely, fight more bravely, and live more fully.

[Krull](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0085811/) is a masterpiece of 80s fantasy, but it is Rell’s quiet, heartbreaking sacrifice that makes it a timeless story about the beautiful, tragic privilege of being alive.
